PROJECT 01 / ENERGY MODELLING

NL grid congestion × solar curtailment What does Dutch grid congestion actually cost a household with rooftop solar?

Pythonpvlib · PVGISpytestFolium / LeafletStreamlit

The question

New Dutch grid rules take effect on 1 July 2026. Congestion is usually discussed as a network-operator problem. Framed from the other end: for a household that already has panels on the roof, what does it cost — in kWh and in euros?

The method

Two layers and a bridge. The spatial layer (boundaries.py, congestion.py) classifies each NL area by afname (offtake) and teruglevering (feed-in) status from the ACM register, normalised to available / limited / under_study / congested with a feed-in severity score of 0–3. The temporal layer (pv_model.py, curtailment.py, knmi.py) models hourly residential PV with pvlib + PVGIS and a KNMI cross-check, detects peak-export hours, and computes lost kWh plus a two-bucket euro loss — netted volume at roughly the retail rate, surplus at the terugleververgoeding, under 2026 full saldering.

What the model outputs

A per-area feed-in severity map and, for a modelled household, the curtailed kWh and the split euro loss behind it. The link from severity to curtailment lives in bridge.py and is an explicit, documented, adjustable assumption — not a measured correlation. It is written down as a scenario dial precisely so a reader can disagree with it and change it. That distinction is the point of the repo.

Open the repository 9 modules in src/ · pytest suite + conftest.py · environment.yml · notebooks/ · streamlit_app.py
Map output is pre-computed to docs/ — no live backend, and no hosted demo to log into.

PROJECT 02 / APPLIED RESEARCH

Does AI actually improve ESG compliance? The compliance-scoring component of an MSc thesis at Erasmus University Rotterdam, extended with a deeper statistical re-analysis.

PythonSQL · SQLiteErasmus RotterdamMSc thesis

The question

AI-assisted ESG reporting tools claim they improve compliance. Do reports produced with them actually score higher against the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) under CSRD? Thesis: "The Sustainability Paradox: How AI is Revolutionizing Sustainability Reporting While Creating New Challenges."

The method

38 EU companies — 19 using AI ESG platforms, paired with 19 matched traditional peers. Their 2024 sustainability reports were scored against the 12 ESRS standards on a 0–3 disclosure-completeness rubric (Morhardt et al., 2002). Scoring was human-led against the ESRS directive, AI-verified, then human-reconciled: ≈95% agreement, Cohen's κ ≈ 0.92.

What the data said

The AI group scored 0.84 points higher on average out of 36 — and the difference is not statistically significant, under either a parametric t-test (p = 0.58) or a non-parametric Wilcoxon test (p = 0.50). Effect size is negligible (Cohen's d = 0.13), and the 95% bootstrap CI on the gap spans [−2.0, +3.6]. A power analysis shows that at n = 19 the study can only detect medium-to-large effects (d ≥ 0.68), so it cannot rule out small effects in either direction. That is the finding, reported as-is.

Open the repository build_csv_from_thesis.py · build_database.py · run_analysis.py · analysis_queries.sql · compliance.db
Written up in FINDINGS.md, SCORING_METHODOLOGY.md and data_provenance.md.

PROJECT 03 / FUNDING STRUCTURE

How do land-use NGOs actually fund themselves? An income-mix benchmark of 14 peer NGOs in regenerative agriculture, nature restoration and sustainable land use.

Jupyter NotebookPythonExcel

The question

"Diversify your funding" is standard advice and almost never quantified. For NGOs working in regenerative agriculture, nature restoration and sustainable land use: where does the money actually come from, and what does the real distribution imply for a diversification strategy?

The method

Built a dataset of 14 peer NGOs' income by source (build_dataset.py → ngo_income_data.xlsx), then analysed the mix in a Jupyter notebook (analysis.ipynb). The notebook itself is generated programmatically by build_notebook.py, so the analysis rebuilds from source rather than living as an untracked, hand-edited file.

What the data said

Business income — corporate sponsors and foundation grants — dominates the larger organisations: NGOs above €5M draw roughly 70% of income from that category, while those under €1M anchor on community engagement (member fees, lease income, programme services). More than half the NGOs studied rely on a single source for over 50% of income — so realistic "diversification" in this sector means one anchor plus two or three secondary streams, not an even split. And zero of the 14 report any venture-capital income.

Open the repository build_dataset.py · ngo_income_data.xlsx · build_notebook.py → analysis.ipynb
