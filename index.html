CASE 01 / FUNDING STRATEGY

RAAP! — Revenue diversification strategy A funding base built from what investors said they would actually fund.

RotterdamJan–Apr 2025Regenerative agriculture

Problem

A Dutch regenerative-agriculture venture needed a resilient funding base — not one more grant cycle, but a revenue structure that could survive donor churn.

Method

Scraped anbi.nl financial statements of Dutch public-benefit organisations and mapped revenue mix, cost structure and donor concentration across 14 peer NGOs. In parallel, ran 20 impact-investor interviews, coded in Atlas.ti using the Gioia methodology.

Adopted outcome

A funding strategy the client adopted — pairing willingness-to-fund evidence from investors with market data from peers, so every recommended revenue stream arrived with proof that someone would pay for it.

ANBI funding-structure analysis on GitHub

CASE 02 / GO-TO-MARKET

Development Alternatives — Biomass go-to-market strategy A district biomass roadmap built from five blocks of field evidence.

DelhiDec 2025–May 2026Biomass enterprise

Problem

A district biomass strategy needed evidence, not assumptions — real adoption barriers and real supply-chain constraints, before anyone committed to a product portfolio.

Method

Field study across 5 administrative blocks on supply-chain bottlenecks and adoption barriers, with 8 interviews coded in Atlas.ti. Enterprise packages standardised for 2 business types — business plan, cost-benefit analysis and financial projections for each. Technology pipeline screened on technological and commercial readiness.

Adopted outcome

10 biomass-linked products structured into a five-year roadmap and go-to-market plan, with a modelled base case of 25% sales growth.

Client engagement · deliverables not public

CASE 03 / APPLIED RESEARCH

Does AI actually improve ESG compliance? CSRD / ESRS master thesis — and the discipline to report a null result.

Erasmus RSM2025–26MSc thesis

Problem

AI ESG-reporting platforms claim they produce better compliance. Is the claim true? Nobody had tested it against the standards themselves.

Method

38 EU companies — 19 AI-platform users against 19 matched peers. Their 2024 reports scored against the 12 ESRS standards on a 0–3 rubric: human-led scoring with AI verification (≈95% agreement, Cohen's κ ≈ 0.92), then parametric and non-parametric re-analysis with a power analysis.

Adopted outcome

An honest null result. The AI group scores marginally higher, but the difference is not statistically significant — and the data cannot rule out small effects. Reported exactly that way, because a clean "no" is a finding.
