PROJECT 01 / LIVE TOOL

India–EU carbon ratings — a Scope 1+2 calculator The FTA removed the tariffs. Carbon is the tariff that's left.

JavaScript95-row factor DBCBAM default valuesAI form-fill (opt.)

The question

The India–EU FTA (concluded 27 January 2026) zeroes tariffs on India's biggest export sectors while CBAM's definitive regime prices carbon at the EU border. What does an Indian supplier's product actually carry, and how does it compare with India's own CBAM default values?

The method

An onboarding wizard (sector · role · activity data) feeds a board that computes Scope 1 + Scope 2 with a full calculation trail: every line is activity × factor, each with its source and vintage. All 95 factors come from a programmatically built database — GHG Protocol v2.0, CEA v21.0, IPCC 2006/AR6, DEFRA 2026 cross-check — none hard-coded. The optional AI step fills the form; it never does the arithmetic.

What it does

A live board: Scope 1, Scope 2 (location-based, vintage-pinned), process emissions, intensity per tonne, and an exposure check against 256 India CN-code default values from the CBAM definitive period — labelled indicative, not a filing. Change an input and stale results clear: a number that no longer matches its inputs is invented. Golden checks are verified by hand and against the source workbooks.

Launch the calculator Browse the source & factor database Ships with SPEC.md · FACTOR_SOURCES.md (full provenance) · factors.csv · CBAM India default values · reproducible build scripts.

PROJECT 02 / CREDIT RISK · ENGINE PUBLIC, UI IN BUILD

CBAM borrower screening for bank risk teams From a borrower list to "who do we call before the February 2027 certificate window."

Python · pydanticsourced YAML rulespytest ×235Streamlit (next)

The question

CBAM's definitive regime gives a bank's corporate borrowers certificate costs, a one-block Feb–Sep 2027 cash-out for the 2026 obligation, and gaps against the bank's own transition ratings. Which borrowers actually matter?

The method

A spec-driven build (CLAUDE.md, tasks with test gates). Every rule, price and factor lives in sourced YAML with an adopted | proposal status — Reg (EU) 2023/956, the 2025 Simplification Regulation, IR 2025/2621 default values, the COM(2026) 616 slower phase-out schedule. The engine: a tier decision tree (precedence 2>1>3>4), certificate-cost model, 2027 liquidity timing, six flag rules. 235 tests, including the hand example 1,000 t × 1.9 tCO₂/t × €80 × 2.5% = €3,800.

What sourcing the rules surfaced

Two brief-breaking findings: the ETS CBAM factor is allocation retained, not phased out — encoding it as written would invert every cost, so a test now guards the two numbers against each other; and electricity moved to Annex II (direct-only). The NGFS Phase V price input is honestly gated — the data sits behind a login, so a strict-xfail test stands guard instead of an invented price path. Analyst UI and the synthetic 300-borrower demo are the next round.

Browse the source Read the build spec Engine + configs + tests are public now · synthetic demo only, no real borrower data · proposals labelled proposal · not affiliated with any bank.

PROJECT 03 / ENERGY MODELLING

NL grid congestion × solar curtailment What does Dutch grid congestion actually cost a household with rooftop solar?

Pythonpvlib · PVGISpytestFolium / LeafletStreamlit

The question

New Dutch grid rules take effect on 1 July 2026. Congestion is usually framed as a network-operator problem; from the other end, for a household with panels already on the roof, what does it cost in kWh and in euros?

The method

Two layers and a bridge. Spatial (boundaries.py, congestion.py): each NL area classified by afname (offtake) and teruglevering (feed-in) status from the ACM register, normalised to available / limited / under_study / congested with a 0–3 feed-in severity score. Temporal (pv_model.py, curtailment.py, knmi.py): hourly residential PV via pvlib + PVGIS with a KNMI cross-check, peak-export hours, lost kWh, and a two-bucket euro loss — netted volume near retail, surplus at the terugleververgoeding, under 2026 full saldering.

What the model outputs

A per-area feed-in severity map and, for a modelled household, the curtailed kWh and the split euro loss behind it. The link from severity to curtailment lives in bridge.py and is an explicit, documented, adjustable assumption — not a measured correlation: a scenario dial a reader can disagree with and change.

Open the repository 9 modules in src/ · pytest suite + conftest.py · environment.yml · notebooks/ · streamlit_app.py
Map output is pre-computed to docs/ — no live backend, and no hosted demo to log into.

PROJECT 04 / APPLIED RESEARCH

Does AI actually improve ESG compliance? The compliance-scoring component of an MSc thesis at Erasmus University Rotterdam, extended with a deeper statistical re-analysis.

PythonSQL · SQLiteErasmus RotterdamMSc thesis

The question

AI-assisted ESG reporting tools claim they improve compliance. Do reports built with them score higher against the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) under CSRD? Thesis: "The Sustainability Paradox: How AI is Revolutionizing Sustainability Reporting While Creating New Challenges."

The method

38 EU companies — 19 using AI ESG platforms, paired with 19 matched traditional peers. Their 2024 sustainability reports scored against the 12 ESRS standards on a 0–3 disclosure-completeness rubric (Morhardt et al., 2002). Scoring was human-led against the ESRS directive, AI-verified, then human-reconciled: ≈95% agreement, Cohen's κ ≈ 0.92.

What the data said

The AI group scored 0.84 points higher out of 36 — not statistically significant under either a t-test (p = 0.58) or Wilcoxon (p = 0.50). Effect size is negligible (Cohen's d = 0.13); the 95% bootstrap CI on the gap spans [−2.0, +3.6]. At n = 19 the study detects only medium-to-large effects (d ≥ 0.68), so small effects in either direction cannot be ruled out. Reported as-is.

Open the repository build_csv_from_thesis.py · build_database.py · run_analysis.py · analysis_queries.sql · compliance.db
Written up in FINDINGS.md, SCORING_METHODOLOGY.md and data_provenance.md.

PROJECT 05 / FUNDING STRUCTURE

How do land-use NGOs actually fund themselves? An income-mix benchmark of 14 peer NGOs in regenerative agriculture, nature restoration and sustainable land use.

Jupyter NotebookPythonExcel

The question

"Diversify your funding" is standard advice and almost never quantified. For NGOs in regenerative agriculture, nature restoration and sustainable land use: where does the money actually come from, and what does that imply for a diversification strategy?

The method

Built a dataset of 14 peer NGOs' income by source (build_dataset.py → ngo_income_data.xlsx), then analysed the mix in a Jupyter notebook (analysis.ipynb). The notebook is generated programmatically by build_notebook.py, so the analysis rebuilds from source rather than living as a hand-edited file.

What the data said

Business income — corporate sponsors and foundation grants — dominates the larger organisations: NGOs above €5M draw roughly 70% from it; those under €1M anchor on community engagement (member fees, lease income, programme services). More than half rely on one source for over 50% of income, so realistic diversification here means one anchor plus two or three secondary streams, not an even split. Zero of the 14 report venture-capital income.

Open the repository build_dataset.py · ngo_income_data.xlsx · build_notebook.py → analysis.ipynb
